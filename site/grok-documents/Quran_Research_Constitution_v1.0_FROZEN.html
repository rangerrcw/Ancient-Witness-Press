QURAN RESEARCH PROJECT

Research Constitution and Standards

Version 1.0 FINAL

Date: August 16, 2026 Status: FINAL — READY FOR OWNER APPROVAL AND FREEZE

I. PURPOSE

The Quran Research Project exists to conduct a systematic, text-centered investigation of the Quran in order to determine as accurately as possible:

What the Quran actually says.

What the Quran claims about God, humanity, revelation, prophets, sin, judgment, salvation, and the afterlife.

How the Arabic language affects interpretation.

How individual passages relate to the Quran as a whole.

How the Quran describes earlier Jewish and Christian revelation.

Where the Quran and Bible agree.

Where they differ.

Where they appear to contradict.

Whether apparent contradictions survive careful linguistic, textual, historical, and contextual examination.

How the Quran portrays biblical people and events.

What later Islamic traditions add to the Quranic text.

What can reasonably be established historically concerning relevant Quranic claims.

How the Quranic text was transmitted and preserved.

How major Quranic teachings develop across the probable chronology of revelation.

The project is an investigation.

It will not begin by assuming either that Islam is true or that Christianity is true.

Its governing principle is:

Let the evidence establish the conclusion.

II. FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH PRINCIPLE

Every major conclusion must distinguish among:

what the Quran explicitly states;

what the Quran strongly implies;

what later Islamic tradition teaches;

what Christian tradition teaches;

what Jewish tradition teaches;

what historical evidence establishes;

what modern scholarship proposes;

and what remains uncertain.

A proposition will not become evidence merely because it is frequently repeated.

III. PRIMARY TEXTS

A. Quran

The primary object of investigation is the Arabic Quran.

English translations are tools for accessing the Arabic text and are not independent textual authorities.

Multiple translations will be consulted whenever translation materially affects interpretation.

Important Arabic terms will be investigated through:

Arabic spelling;

transliteration;

root where relevant;

morphology where relevant;

semantic range;

Quranic usage;

immediate context;

parallel occurrences;

historical linguistic evidence where useful;

and translation choices.

B. Bible

Whenever comparison with the Bible is relevant, the biblical text itself will be investigated.

Where interpretation depends upon language, relevant:

Hebrew;

Aramaic;

and Greek

evidence will be examined.

Bible/Quran comparisons should therefore compare underlying textual evidence whenever practical rather than merely comparing English translations.

IV. TEXT VERSUS INTERPRETATION

The project must preserve distinctions between different evidentiary layers.

Level 1 — Quranic Text

What the Quran explicitly states.

Level 2 — Quranic Internal Context

What other Quranic passages contribute to understanding the passage.

Level 3 — Early Islamic Sources

Hadith, Sirah, early Tafsir, and other early Islamic material.

Level 4 — Classical Islamic Interpretation

Major later Tafsir, jurisprudence, theology, and interpretive traditions.

Level 5 — Modern Interpretation and Scholarship

Contemporary Muslim, Christian, Jewish, secular, historical, linguistic, and academic scholarship.

Later interpretation may illuminate the Quran but must never silently replace the Quran itself.

V. EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

Every significant conclusion will receive an evidence classification.

🟢 DIRECT TEXTUAL STATEMENT

Explicitly stated by the primary text being examined.

🔵 STRONG TEXTUAL PATTERN

Not contained in one explicit statement but strongly supported by multiple passages or a consistent textual pattern.

🟡 CAREFUL INTERPRETIVE SYNTHESIS

A reasonable interpretation or synthesis supported by evidence but not explicitly stated.

🟠 OPEN RESEARCH QUESTION

Evidence remains insufficient, ambiguous, disputed, or incomplete.

These classifications measure strength of evidence, not whether a conclusion favors Christianity, Islam, Judaism, secular scholarship, or another position.

VII. BIBLE–QURAN CROSS-SCRIPTURE COMPARISON ATLAS

A permanent Cross-Scripture Comparison Atlas will track significant relationships between the Quran and Bible.

Comparisons may be classified:

AGREEMENT

The texts make substantially compatible claims.

PARTIAL AGREEMENT

Important common elements exist alongside meaningful differences.

ADDITION

The Quran contains information absent from the biblical account.

OMISSION

Significant material appearing in one account is absent from the other.

REINTERPRETATION

A person, event, doctrine, or concept receives significantly different theological treatment.

APPARENT CONTRADICTION

The texts initially appear incompatible but additional investigation is required.

DIRECT CONTRADICTION

After reasonable linguistic, contextual, chronological, historical, and interpretive alternatives have been investigated, the texts make mutually incompatible claims.

A difference is not automatically a contradiction.

final determination;

related discoveries;

related Dictionary entries;

and related research questions.

IDs are permanent and never reused.

IX. CONTRADICTION PROTOCOL

Before declaring a direct contradiction, investigate:

Original-language wording.

Immediate context.

Broader textual context.

Whether the passages address the same issue.

Translation differences.

Semantic range.

Historical context.

Cultural context.

Chronology.

Genre and rhetorical purpose.

Reasonable Muslim interpretation.

Reasonable Christian/Jewish interpretation where applicable.

Relevant historical evidence.

Only after these possibilities have been investigated may a finding become a Direct Contradiction.

The standard must operate equally in both directions.

X. INTERNAL QURANIC COMPARISON

The Quran will continually be compared with itself.

Research will identify:

repeated narratives;

repeated phrases;

thematic development;

different presentations of events;

theological patterns;

legal developments;

apparent tensions;

and possible abrogation.

XI. INTERNAL TENSION REGISTER

Potential Quran-vs-Quran tensions receive permanent identifiers:

QI-0001, QI-0002, QI-0003…

A QI record begins as an apparent tension, not a contradiction.

Each record should investigate:

relevant passages;

Arabic wording;

contexts;

probable chronology;

translation;

traditional explanations;

abrogation arguments where relevant;

alternative interpretations;

and final assessment.

Possible final classifications include:

harmonized;

complementary;

development;

abrogation-related;

unresolved;

apparent contradiction;

or direct internal contradiction.

XII. CHRONOLOGY

Primary research will proceed in canonical Quran order: Surah 1 through Surah 114.

Probable revelation chronology will simultaneously be recorded as metadata.

Relevant passages should be classified where supportable as:

Meccan;

Medinan;

disputed;

or uncertain.

Chronological claims must state their degree of certainty.

After canonical research is completed, the accumulated evidence may be reconstructed chronologically to examine theological, legal, and rhetorical development.

XIII. ABROGATION

The Islamic concept of naskh will receive a dedicated research track.

Investigate:

whether the Quran teaches abrogation;

relevant Arabic terminology;

passages traditionally used as evidence;

historically proposed abrogated passages;

competing Islamic interpretations;

chronological relationships;

and implications for Quranic interpretation.

Later theories must not automatically be attributed to the Quran.

XIV. PARALLEL TRANSLATION MATRIX

Whenever translation materially affects meaning, a Parallel Translation Matrix will be created.

It should include representative translations sufficient to expose meaningful differences.

Research then returns to the Arabic text to determine why translations differ.

Translation selection must never be manipulated to favor a predetermined conclusion.

Significant translation differences become part of the permanent research record.

XV. QURAN RESEARCH DICTIONARY

A cumulative Arabic research dictionary will be maintained.

Entries should contain where relevant:

Arabic spelling;

transliteration;

root;

morphology;

basic meaning;

semantic range;

Quranic occurrences;

contextual usage;

significant translation differences;

theological importance;

related Hebrew/Greek terms when genuinely comparable;

discovery relationships;

and cross-references.

Definitions must emerge from evidence rather than modern assumptions.

XVI. NARRATIVE SOURCE/PARALLEL TRACKER

Whenever a Quranic narrative parallels material found elsewhere, relevant earlier or contemporary sources may be investigated.

Potential sources include:

Hebrew Bible;

New Testament;

Jewish interpretive traditions;

rabbinic literature;

Christian apocrypha;

Syriac Christian literature;

late-antique traditions;

Arabian traditions;

and other historically relevant material.

Similarity alone does not demonstrate borrowing.

Historical dependency requires evidence.

Each finding must distinguish:

similarity;

possible relationship;

probable relationship;

and demonstrable dependency.

XVII. HISTORICAL CONTEXT

Relevant historical investigation may include:

pre-Islamic Arabia;

Arabian Judaism;

Arabian Christianity;

Byzantine Christianity;

Syriac Christianity;

Jewish communities;

religious practices;

political events;

geography;

languages;

trade;

and surrounding late-antique culture.

Historical reconstruction must remain distinguishable from Quranic statements.

XVIII. HISTORICAL CLAIM AUDIT

Historically testable Quranic claims may receive formal investigation.

Each audit should consider available:

archaeological evidence;

manuscript evidence;

inscriptions;

contemporary or near-contemporary literature;

geography;

chronology;

and relevant historical scholarship.

Possible classifications include:

historically supported;

consistent with available evidence;

plausible but unverified;

insufficient evidence;

disputed;

in tension with available evidence;

or contradicted by strong historical evidence.

Absence of evidence must not automatically be treated as evidence of absence.

XIX. MANUSCRIPT AND TEXTUAL HISTORY

A permanent research track will examine the transmission and preservation of the Quran.

Subjects include:

oral transmission;

early written material;

traditional collection accounts;

Abu Bakr;

Uthman;

standardization;

early manuscripts;

consonantal text;

diacritics;

qira’at;

canonical readings;

textual variants;

manuscript dating;

and claims of perfect preservation.

Traditional Islamic accounts, physical manuscript evidence, and modern scholarship must remain distinguishable.

XX. SOURCE/TRADITION PROVENANCE LEDGER

Every important extra-Quranic claim must identify its source category.

Possible categories include:

QURAN HADITH SIRAH TAFSIR FIQH/JURISPRUDENCE CLASSICAL ISLAMIC THEOLOGY MODERN ISLAMIC INTERPRETATION BIBLE JEWISH TRADITION CHRISTIAN TRADITION HISTORICAL EVIDENCE MODERN ACADEMIC SCHOLARSHIP

This prevents statements originating in later tradition from silently becoming statements attributed to the Quran.

XXI. HADITH, SIRAH, AND TAFSIR

Hadith, Sirah, and Tafsir are valuable historical and interpretive sources but are not automatically Quranic evidence.

Whenever they are used, provenance must be explicitly identified.

Where authenticity, dating, transmission, or interpretation is disputed, that uncertainty must be recorded.

XXII. CLAIMS AUDIT

Common claims about Islam, Christianity, the Quran, or Bible will not automatically be accepted.

Important claims receive permanent identifiers:

QCA-0001, QCA-0002, QCA-0003…

Examples might include:

“The Bible was corrupted.”

“The Quran has never changed.”

“The Quran says Jesus was not crucified.”

“Muhammad is predicted in the Bible.”

“Islam and Christianity worship exactly the same conception of God.”

Each audit will determine:

Precisely what is being claimed.

Where the claim originates.

Whether the Quran actually states it.

Relevant Arabic evidence.

Relevant biblical evidence.

Relevant Islamic traditional evidence.

Relevant historical evidence.

Strong arguments supporting the claim.

Strong arguments opposing the claim.

Final evidence classification.

Claims may remain unresolved.

XXV. ARGUMENT FAIRNESS STANDARD

Important disputed issues must be investigated using the strongest reasonable interpretations available.

No position may be rejected through an obviously weak version of its argument.

The governing question is:

What is the strongest evidence supporting each interpretation, and which explanation best accounts for all available evidence?

This applies equally to:

Muslim claims;

Christian claims;

Jewish claims;

secular claims;

and academic theories.

XXVI. UNCERTAINTY STANDARD

The project must be willing to conclude:

The available evidence does not presently establish an answer.

Uncertainty is preferable to manufactured certainty.

Open questions remain active and may be revisited when additional evidence emerges later in the Quran.

XXIX. CHECKPOINT ARCHITECTURE

Because Quranic surahs vary enormously in length, checkpoints will not operate simply every fixed number of surahs.

Checkpoint boundaries will primarily reflect:

verse volume;

research volume;

thematic complexity;

and preservation requirements.

The system will use two levels:

Incremental Checkpoints

Created after a manageable body of new research has accumulated.

They preserve all new substantive research since the previous checkpoint.

Cumulative Integration Checkpoints

Created at predetermined major milestones.

They integrate and verify the cumulative repository.

Before Surah 1 begins, the initial milestone schedule will be established.

After that schedule is frozen, it will not be casually redesigned during the project.

Every checkpoint requires:

completeness verification;

permanent-ID verification;

cross-reference verification;

continuity verification;

and confirmation that no substantive discoveries remain only in chat.

XXX. FINAL CLAIM-TO-EVIDENCE TRACEABILITY

Every major conclusion appearing in the final Comprehensive Quran Research Synthesis must trace backward to the evidence establishing it.

Where applicable, conclusions should connect to:

QDISC records;

QB records;

QI records;

QCA records;

Quranic passages;

Arabic analysis;

biblical passages;

original biblical-language analysis;

historical audits;

provenance records;

and scholarly sources.

No major conclusion may suddenly appear at the end of the project without having been established in the underlying research.

XXXI. SOURCE HIERARCHY

Research should prioritize approximately:

Quranic Arabic text.

Internal Quranic evidence.

Biblical Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek where relevant.

Manuscripts and primary historical evidence.

Early Islamic sources.

Classical Islamic scholarship.

Relevant Jewish and Christian historical sources.

Peer-reviewed/respected modern scholarship.

Modern Muslim/Christian/Jewish scholarship.

General reference material.

Popular apologetics or polemics may identify research questions but do not constitute final proof.

XXXII. SEPARATION FROM THE THOUGHT PROJECT

The Quran Research Project remains separate from The Thought Project.

Findings from the Quran will not silently modify conclusions established during Bible research.

Connections will instead be recorded through the Quran project’s comparison systems.

This protects the evidentiary integrity of both projects.

After all 114 surahs have been investigated, the repository should allow evidence-based answers to questions including:

What does the Quran actually teach?

How does the Quran describe God?

Who is Jesus according to the Quran?

Who is Muhammad according to the Quran?

What is salvation according to the Quran?

What is sin according to the Quran?

What does the Quran say concerning previous Scripture?

Does the Quran teach textual corruption of previous Scripture?

How does Quranic biblical history compare with the Bible?

Where do Christianity and Islam genuinely agree?

Where do they partially agree?

Where do they fundamentally disagree?

Which alleged contradictions disappear under investigation?

Which remain unresolved?

Which constitute genuine textual incompatibilities?

How internally consistent is the Quran?

What role does abrogation play?

How does Quranic teaching develop chronologically?

What is historically known about Quranic transmission?

What does manuscript evidence demonstrate concerning preservation?

Which commonly repeated claims about Islam or the Quran are supported by evidence?

Which are unsupported or overstated?

Which conclusions are firmly established?

Which remain uncertain?

Every major answer should be traceable through the repository to its supporting evidence.

XXXIV. GOVERNANCE AND FREEZE RULE

Once the owner explicitly approves this Constitution:

THE CONSTITUTION BECOMES FROZEN.

After freezing:

No new tracking systems will be casually introduced.

No new research repositories will be created simply because another organization later appears attractive.

No established ID systems will be replaced.

No evidence standards will be silently changed.

No required research categories will be silently dropped.

No substantive simplification will occur without owner approval.

A genuinely unforeseen defect may require correction, but it must be:

identified explicitly;

explained;

evaluated for effects upon previous research;

approved by the owner;

documented as a formal amendment;

and applied consistently.

New information belongs inside the architecture.

New information does not automatically justify new architecture.

XXXV. NO-MIDSTREAM-REDESIGN RULE

The planning stage ends when this Constitution and the initial checkpoint schedule are frozen.

Once Surah 1 research begins, the project’s purpose changes from:

designing the research system

to:

executing the research system.

A new discovery that does not perfectly fit an anticipated category should first be accommodated within the existing repository and cross-reference architecture.

The project will not repeatedly redesign itself while research is underway.

XXXVI. FINAL QUALITY STANDARD

The Quran Research Project must be:

Text-centered. Original-language aware. Historically informed. Source-transparent. Cross-referenced. Cumulative. Recoverable. Evidence-classified. Fair to competing interpretations. Explicit about uncertainty. Consistent from Surah 1 through Surah 114. And completely traceable from final conclusion back to evidence.

XXXVII. FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLE

Read what the text says.

Examine what the original language permits.

Determine where a claim actually originates.

Compare claims with the relevant evidence.

Represent competing interpretations fairly.

Distinguish text from tradition.

Distinguish evidence from inference.

Record disagreements rather than hiding them.

Admit uncertainty when the evidence is uncertain.

Follow the evidence wherever it leads.
