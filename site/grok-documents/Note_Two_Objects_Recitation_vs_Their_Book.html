NOTE — THIS RECITATION VS THEIR BOOK

Question: Was the Quran he was using unaltered? Did he previously claim it was already altered?

IDs: QDISC-0513–0514; QB-0286

Date: 14 September 2026

Do not let the pronoun float

If ‘it’ means the Recitation he is delivering: the walked Quran never says that Recitation is already corrupted. It says the opposite about his hands: he may not change it (10:15). If ‘it’ means the Tawrāt / Injīl: the walked Quran charges mishandling (distort, hide, write-with-hands) and still tells them to fetch, recite, judge by, and uphold those named books. Two objects. Two claims.

Object 1 — this Recitation

10:15–16: he cannot change it of his own accord; he follows what is revealed; he lived among them before it. 7:203: he does not produce a sign on demand. 6:50: he follows what is revealed. Level 1 of this text: the thing coming out of his mouth is claimed as protected from his editorial will. That is a claim about him while he recites. It is not a laboratory certificate for every later copy.

Object 2 — the Book with them

2:75, 2:79, 4:46, 5:13, 5:41, 6:91: a party heard then distorted; some write the book with their hands; they hide much; they forgot a portion. 3:93 / 5:43–47 / 5:66–68 / 7:157 / 9:111: bring it, judge by it, uphold it, find him written in it, a promise binding in it. The walked file is mishandling plus still-a-witness. It is not ‘those books are nothing and already gone.’

So: did he claim ‘it’ was already altered?

He did not claim the Recitation he was reciting was already altered. He claimed other communities mishandle their Book — and he still pointed at that Book as a witness. Later Islam often hardened that into tahrif al-lafz (the wording itself replaced). That hardening is later. It is also in tension with the fetch-and-uphold verses already locked.
