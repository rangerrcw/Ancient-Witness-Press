BIBLICAL DIVORCE LAW — COMPARISON NOTE

Companion to S002 CP14 and QB-0055. Describes texts. Not a procedure manual.

Date: 11 September 2026

1. What Deuteronomy 24:1–4 actually legislates

The case: a man marries; she finds no favor because he found ʿervat dāvār (“nakedness of a thing” / some indecency) in her; he writes a sefer keritut (certificate of cutting-off), puts it in her hand, and sends her from his house. She becomes another man’s wife. That second husband dislikes her and also writes a bill and sends her out, or he dies. Then: her first husband may not take her again to be his wife after she has been defiled — that is tōʿēbāh before the Lord.

Grammatically the command is verse 4. Verses 1–3 are the situation. Many interpreters therefore say Moses is not “commanding divorce” so much as regulating a practice already happening and closing one door: no return to husband #1 after husband #2.

ʿErvat dāvār is the disputed phrase. Second Temple and rabbinic schools split on it: Shammai heard sexual indecency; Hillel heard something as small as spoiling a dish (m. Gittin 9:10). That debate is what the Pharisees bring to Jesus.

2. Other Torah pieces that are not Deut 24

Exod 21:7–11: a female servant-wife has food, clothing, and conjugal rights; if those are withdrawn she goes out free. Not a general divorce statute.

Deut 22:13–21: false accusation of missing virginity — he may not divorce her all his days. Deut 22:28–29: intercourse with an unbetrothed virgin — bride-price and he may not send her away all his days. These are anti-divorce locks in specific cases.

Lev 21:7, 14: priests may not take a divorced woman. A holiness fence, not a general ban on lay divorce.

Deut 25:5–10: levirate. A widow without a son is bound toward the brother-in-law’s line. Different institution from 2:234’s numbered widow clock.

There is no numbered ʿiddah of three qurūʾ or four-months-ten in the Torah. There is no “divorce is twice then a third.” There is no khulʿ ransom clause.

3. Prophets

Jer 3:1, 8 uses Deut 24 as a metaphor: Israel played the harlot; after sending her away, can the first husband return? God still calls return — the metaphor strains the statute.

Isa 50:1: where is your mother’s bill of divorce? — God denying he divorced Zion in that legal sense.

Mal 2:13–16: Judah has been faithless to the wife of his youth; “I hate divorce” (or: he hates divorce / covering one’s garment with violence — the Hebrew of 2:16 is rough). This is moral indictment of men discarding Jewish wives, in the same Persian-period file as Ezra–Nehemiah’s campaign against foreign marriages. Two pressures sit in one era: do not dump the covenant wife; do not keep the foreign wife.

4. Jesus

Matt 19:3–9 / Mark 10:2–12. Pharisees: is it lawful to divorce for any cause? Jesus quotes Gen 1:27 and 2:24 — male and female; one flesh; what God joined let no one separate. They cite Moses’ certificate. He: because of hardness of heart Moses allowed it; from the beginning it was not so. Matthew adds: whoever divorces except for porneia and marries another commits adultery. Mark has no exception and includes the woman who divorces her husband (more Roman-legal framing).

Matt 5:31–32 in the Sermon: the certificate saying is not enough; divorce except for porneia causes adultery in the subsequent union.

Luke 16:18: no exception clause in that saying.

So inside the NT itself: a creation-standard (no separation), a hardness-concession reading of Deut 24, and a Matthean porneia exception. Churches still split on whether that exception is adultery only, a wider sexual-sin set, or invalid-union language.

5. Paul

1 Cor 7:10–16. Not I but the Lord: a wife should not separate; if she does, remain unmarried or be reconciled; a husband should not divorce. Then Paul’s own word: if an unbelieving spouse departs, the brother or sister is not enslaved. Mixed-marriage desertion is a second track. No ʿiddah clock. No third-talaq-plus-intervening-marriage device.

7. What not to claim

Do not say “the Bible bans divorce.” Deut 24 exists. Do not say “the Quran copies Deut 24.” The intervening-marriage rule runs the other way. Do not say “Jesus and 2:229 teach the same ethic.” Jesus’ creation-standard plus Matthew’s porneia exception is a different architecture from twice-then-third plus khulʿ plus ʿiddah.

Rabbinic get procedure, Catholic annulment, Protestant fault/no-fault statutes, and Islamic ṭalāq-bidʿī debates are all later law. They are not Level 1 for either book.

8. Bottom line for the project

Biblical divorce law is a small Torah case (Deut 24) plus anti-divorce locks in a few situations, a prophetic protest against treacherous sending-away, and a NT tightening toward creation-monogamy with an internal split on exceptions. Quran 2:228–237 is a thicker code: numbered waits, a two-then-third cap, a ransom off-ramp, a required intervening marriage after the third, nursing and widow clocks, mutʿa. The one place the two statutes collide head-on is the second-husband rule.
