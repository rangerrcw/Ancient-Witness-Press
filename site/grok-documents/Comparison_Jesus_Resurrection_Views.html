COMPARISON NOTE — JESUS RESURRECTION / RAISING VIEWS

Pair with: Comparison_Quranic_Crucifixion_Views.docx

IDs: QB-0103–0106

Date: 11 September 2026

Resurrection and raising are not the same word-set. The Gospels narrate death, burial, third-day rising, appearances, then ascension. The Quran narrates a raise-to-God (rafaʿa ilayhi) and a future “day I die / day I am raised.” Mixing those two plots as if they were one plot is the usual confusion. This note keeps them apart.

1. Level 1 — Quran verbs

4:158. Bal rafaʿahu llāhu ilayhi — rather, God raised him to Himself.

3:55. Innī mutawaffīka wa-rāfiʿuka ilayya — I am taking you / causing you to die and raising you to Me.

5:117. When You tawaffaytanī You were the watcher over them.

19:33. Peace on me the day I was born, the day I die (amūtu), and the day I am raised alive (ubʿathu ḥayyan).

19:15. Same three-beat formula for John.

4:159. None of the People of the Book but will believe in him before his death; he will be a witness against them at the Rising (yawm al-qiyāma).

The Quran does not narrate: a burial; a third day; appearances in a locked room; breakfast by the lake; forty days; a cloud-ascent from Olivet. Those are Gospel/Acts scenes. Do not insert them under rafaʿa.

Baʿatha / yubʿathu in 19:33 is the ordinary resurrection-of-the-dead verb. Rafaʿa ilayhi is raise-toward-God. Two verbs. Two slots in the timeline if you accept the majority reading (raise now, die later, baʿth later still).

2. What rafaʿa does in the Quran generally

Rafaʿa can mean lift in space, lift in rank, or both. Idrīs is described as raised to a high place (19:57). God raises degrees (6:83). The throne is raised. So “God raised Jesus to Himself” can be heard as bodily relocation, as honor, or as both. The majority tradition heard bodily-alive relocation because 4:157 had just denied the kill. Honor-only is a minority / Ahmadi-leaning hearing (rafʿ as dignity, not flight). Level 1 is the verb plus ilayhi. It does not photograph the travel.

The descent-as-judge, breaking the cross, killing the pig, abolishing jizya: that list is ḥadīth (e.g. Bukhārī / Muslim reports). Level 4 for this project unless and until a Quran verse states it. 43:61 is sometimes read as Jesus being a sign of the Hour — wording argued.

4. Gospel / Acts plot

Death on Friday. Burial. Third-day empty tomb. Appearances (Mary Magdalene; the eleven; Thomas; the road; the lake). Eating. Wounds shown. Commission. Luke–Acts: taken up after forty days from Olivet; will return the same way. Paul: if Christ is not raised, preaching is empty (1 Cor 15). The raising is of one who had died. The return is of one who already died and rose.

QB-0103 CONTRAST of plot: Gospel = died-then-raised-then-ascended-then-will-return. Majority Islam = did-not-die-then-raised-alive-then-will-return-then-will-die-then-general-baʿth.

QB-0104 PARTIAL AGREEMENT: both files say Jesus is not in the grave the crowd thought they owned, and that God acted. Both files expect him to be involved at the end (Gospel return; Islamic descent tradition; 4:159 witness at the Rising).

QB-0105 CONTRAST of work: in Paul the resurrection of Jesus is firstfruits and the ground of everyone else’s rising. In the Quran Jesus raises the dead by leave as a sign in his mission (3:49; 5:110); his own baʿth in 19:33 is grouped with every human’s three days (birth / death / rising). He is not named as the engine of other people’s resurrection.

5. 19:33 and John 19:15

Infant Jesus and infant John both get: peace the day I/he was born, the day I/he die(s), the day I/he am/is raised alive. That is a human three-beat. Majority Islam hears Jesus’ middle beat as still future (so that 4:157 can stand). A death-then-raise reading hears 19:33 as already using the same three-beat every human uses. The verse itself does not date the middle beat. QB-0100 remains OPEN.

6. 4:159 again

Believe in him before his death. If “his” is Jesus, the verse is usually parked on the descent-then-death plot. If “his” is each kitābī, the verse is about dying faith, not about a future public return. Both hearings are old. The resurrection comparison cannot lean on 4:159 until that pronoun is argued in place.
