NOTE — 10:99–100 THREE CLAIMS, ONE TENSION

IDs: QDISC-0560; QB-0305

Date: 14 September 2026

The two verses

10:99 If your Lord had willed, whoever is in the earth would have believed, all of them together. Will you then compel the people until they become believers?

10:100 It is not for a soul to believe except by God’s leave. He places the rijs on those who do not understand.

Three claims, not one slogan

A. God could have made every person on earth a believer, all at once, if He had willed. He has not so willed. Unbelief in the world is therefore inside His permission, not a surprise to Him.

B. The Messenger is not to compel people into belief. His job in this stretch is recitation, warning, not forcing the inner act. 10:108 he is not a wakīl over them. Same pole.

C. No soul believes except by God’s leave (idhn). Rijs is placed on those who do not understand. Belief is not an autonomous human product in this verse.

Why it feels like a knot

B sounds like people must be left free. C sounds like they cannot believe unless He permits. A sounds like He could have ended the question and did not. Later schools pull the knot one way: some stress His will so hard that human calling looks theatrical; some stress the no-compel so hard that idhn becomes a rubber stamp after the fact. Level 1 does not pick a school. It leaves the three sentences on the page.

What the verses do clearly: they limit the Messenger. He may want them all to believe. He may not force that result. The result, when it comes, is by leave. The result, when it does not come, is not because God lacked power.
