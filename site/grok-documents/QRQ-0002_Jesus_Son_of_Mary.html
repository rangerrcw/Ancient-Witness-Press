MAJOR-FIGURE DOSSIER: JESUS SON OF MARY

This is a corpus-level QRQ-0002 investigation, requested 11 September 2026. It pulls Quranic Jesus passages that have not all been reached yet in the surah-by-surah packets (packets currently end at 2:82). Sequential Baqarah work continues at 2:83, where Jesus first appears in canonical order (2:87). Until those packets are written, statements below are Level 1 relative to the cited verses, not relative to completed checkpoints.

Arabic name: ʿĪsā ibn Maryam

Titles in the Quran: al-Masīḥ (the Messiah); rasūl (messenger); nabī (prophet); kalimat Allāh / a word from Him; rūḥ from Him; ʿabd Allāh (servant of God); supported by rūḥ al-qudus

Not in the Quran as Level 1: Second Person of the Trinity; “Son of God” as ontology; atoning death; empty tomb narrative; substitution look-alike named; detailed Sermon on the Mount; Last Supper

Method reminder

Hadith about Jesus’ second coming, killing the Dajjāl, breaking the cross, are HADITH. They are not smuggled into “the Quran says.” Injīl in the Quran is the revelation given to Jesus, not automatically the four canonical Gospels on a shelf.

2. What the Quran explicitly affirms about him

Born of Mary without a human father (19:20–21; 3:47). Called Messiah. A messenger and prophet to the Children of Israel. Confirms the Torah. Given the Injīl. Supported by the holy spirit. Performs signs by God’s permission: speaks as an infant, shapes a clay bird that becomes a bird, heals the blind and the leper, raises the dead. Has disciples. Is a word from God cast to Mary and a spirit from Him. Is compared to Adam as a created being. Does not disdain servanthood. Will be involved in eschatological witness (4:159; 43:61 if read of him).

3. What the Quran explicitly denies or forbids saying

That God is the Messiah (5:17, 5:72). That God is “third of three” (5:73). That God has a son (4:171; 19:35; 9:30). That people should take Jesus and Mary as two gods besides God (5:116 — Jesus is depicted denying he taught this). That “they” (the speakers in 4:157, conventionally the Jews in context) killed him or crucified him.

4. The death / crucifixion cluster (do not flatten)

4:157 is the load-bearing verse: mā qatalūhu wa-mā ṣalabūhu wa-lākin shubbiha lahum. They did not kill him and they did not crucify him; it was made to appear to them. Then: they did not kill him yaqīnan. 4:158: God raised him to Himself.

Other verses use tawaffā (3:55; 5:117), which in Quranic usage often means “take in death,” sometimes “take.” 19:33 has Jesus speak of the day he dies and the day he is raised alive — same triad as John the Baptist in 19:15.

Level 1: 4:157 denies that the boastful killers succeeded in killing/crucifying him, and asserts appearance-to-them plus God’s raising. Level 4 majority Islam: no crucifixion, often a substitute. Level 4 minority / some modern readers: he was not successfully killed; death language elsewhere is real or future. Substitute-person stories are TAFSIR/HADITH, not in 4:157’s wording.

OPEN inside the Quranic set: how 4:157 relates to 3:55 and 19:33 without remainder.

5. Word and Spirit — do not baptize the terms

4:171: rasūl Allāh wa-kalimatuhu alqāhā ilā Maryama wa-rūḥun minhu. Messenger, His word that He cast to Mary, and a spirit from Him. 3:45: a word from Him. This is the closest lexical overlap with Johannine Logos. The same verses immediately forbid “Three” and sonship. So the Quran can use “word” and “spirit from Him” without accepting Nicene identity. Christian readers hear John 1. Quranic argument hears creative speech (kun) like Adam (3:59). Both hearings must be recorded. Classification: PARTIAL AGREEMENT on elevated titles; DIRECT DENIAL of the metaphysical use those titles have in mainstream Christology.

6. Mary

Named, chosen, guarded chastity, mother of Jesus, called ṣiddīqa (5:75), eats food (anti-divinity argument). Not called co-deity by the Quran; 5:116 treats worship of Mary as a charge Jesus rejects. Christian practice of asking Mary’s intercession is not the same as calling her a god; the Quranic courtroom scene is still a polemic against taking her as ilāh.

7. Ahmad / Paraclete claim (61:6)

Jesus is quoted announcing a messenger after him named Aḥmad. Islamic reading: Muhammad. Christian counter: NT Paraclete is the Holy Spirit (John 14–16), not a human named Ahmad. Whether Greek paraklētos was ever “periklutos / Ahmad” is a later apologetic construction, not a Quranic argument. Level 1: the Quran attributes to Jesus a prediction of Aḥmad. Historical-critical link to John 14 is OPEN / not demonstrated.

9. What is not a Quranic Jesus finding

Second coming details, descent at a minaret, marriage, grave in Kashmir (Ahmadi), Gospel of Barnabas. Those are later. 43:61 as “sign of the Hour” is a live grammatical debate (pronoun hu).

10. Evidence summary for QRQ-0002

The Quranic Jesus is a virgin-born, miracle-working Messiah, Word and Spirit from God, messenger to Israel, confirmer of Torah, giver of Injīl, servant not God, not a son in the ontological sense, not one of three gods, not successfully killed or crucified by his enemies on the Quran’s 4:157 wording, raised by God to Himself. That profile overlaps the NT on birth, Messiahship, miracles, and Israel mission. It parts on deity, Trinity, atoning death, and the crucifixion as accomplished fact.

Strongest Direct Contradictions with mainstream Christian confession: deity/sonship (QB-0021) and crucifixion-as-death (QB-0023), the second with a narrow remaining ambiguity about tawaffā.

Strongest Agreements: virgin birth (QB-0018); Messiah title; some miracle types.
